A double coat

1

The coat

Two layers. Outer layer: longer, coarser guard hairs. Underneath: a soft, dense undercoat. Labrador Retriever, both Corgis, Border Collie.

2

Its schedule

Brushing
De-shed once a week with an undercoat rake; a few brushing sessions a week suit many dogs
Bathing
Occasional. Roughly every month to every three months.
Groomer
Not essential. A groomer can help with de-shedding.
Every coat
Nails every three to four weeks, ears at each groom, teeth daily if you can

Slicker brush

A starting tool for a double coat, for dense areas

What it does: fine wire bristles for working out tangles and removing loose, dead hair.

Suits: long, silky and curly coats, dense coats that tangle, and the densest areas of double coats.

Limitation: use it gently, especially near the skin. A slicker is for small tangles, not tight mats.

What changed and why

Structure, headings, palette

Structure

The old wireframe stacked eight live sections and attached a Desk feature to each. Now the page has three parts: ask, browse by dog, and a four-chapter reference shelf. Cards, the carousel and icon tiles are gone. Each chapter has one job and one interactive piece.

One thread

The coat picked in the Coat chapter re-lights the plate in the Tools chapter. That is the only place coat changes the advice, so it is the only place the page reacts to it.

Each accent colours only its chapter’s heading, controls and thumb tab, on a light ground of the same hue. Copper stays the only action colour, and red stays reserved for vet lines. Every text pair passes WCAG AA, from 5.4:1 to 8.8:1.

Motion (Emil Kowalski rules)

Results appear in under 150ms. The slip slides in on a drawer curve. Plate tools change colour in 180ms when the coat changes, buttons press to 0.97, and hover applies only to fine pointers. All of it stops under reduced motion.
