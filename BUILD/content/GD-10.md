# Grooming tools: what each one does and which you need

*[Intent line]* You want to buy the few tools your dog's coat actually needs, not a drawer full of gadgets, and to know which ones need extra care.

*[Byline]* By Grooming Dogs · Updated *[publish date]*

*[Scope note]* This page is about **choosing** tools. How to use each one is covered in its own guide, linked in each section. Not sure what coat your dog has? [Identify your dog's coat first](/dog-coat-types/).

*[Lead image `lead-tools` (credit line only)]*

*[Short answer box]*
**Short answer:** Most dogs need a short list of tools:
- **A brush that suits their coat, plus a comb** to check your work **[S3][S4]**;
- **dog nail clippers or a grinder,** with styptic powder **[S3][S5]**;
- **a dog shampoo and a towel** **[S1][S3]**;
- **a soft toothbrush and pet toothpaste** **[S3][S12]**.

Add an undercoat rake for double coats **[S3]**, and blunt-ended scissors or small clippers with guide combs only if you'll do trims at home **[S3]**. A dryer is optional **[S3]**. Your vet or groomer can suggest the right tools for your dog's coat **[S1]**.

---

## How to choose grooming tools

Start from your dog, not the shop shelf:

- **Coat type decides the brush.** The right brushing tools depend on the coat **[S1][S4]**. Find your dog's coat group in [identify your dog's coat first](/dog-coat-types/), then use the starter kits at the bottom of this page.
- **Buy for the jobs you'll actually do.** If a groomer handles haircuts, you don't need clippers.
- **Choose tools that fit comfortably in your hand** so you can work gently and safely **[S2]**.
- **Buy tools made for dogs** where it matters, especially nail clippers, toothpaste and shampoo **[S1][S3][S5][S12]**.
- **Ask for advice.** Your vet or groomer can recommend tools for your dog's coat type **[S1]**.

## Brushes and combs

Most dogs need one main brush and a comb. Which brush depends on the coat. How to use them: see [how to brush your dog properly](/brushing-your-dog/).

### Slicker brush

**What it does:** fine wire bristles for working out tangles and removing loose, dead hair **[S1][S4]**.
**Suits:** long, silky and curly coats **[S3]**, dense coats that tangle **[S4]**, and the densest areas of double coats **[S2]**.
**Limitation:** use it gently, especially near the skin. A slicker is for small tangles, not tight mats. Tight mats need a groomer (see [dealing with mats](/brushing-your-dog/#dealing-with-mats)).

### Pin brush

**What it does:** a brush with spaced pins, used on longer coats **[S1]**.
**Suits:** many long-haired dogs **[S1]**.
**Limitation:** on its own it doesn't check down to the skin. Follow with a comb.

### Bristle brush

**What it does:** removes loose, dead hair and smooths the coat **[S4]**.
**Suits:** short and medium coats, and some long coats **[S1]**. It's often used after a slicker or rubber brush **[S4]**.
**Limitation:** it works on the surface, so it won't get through tangles in a long or double coat.

### Rubber curry brush

**What it does:** a rubber brush that loosens dead hair, dirt and dead skin, and polishes smooth coats **[S1][S4]**. It also helps spread the coat's natural oils **[S3]**.
**Suits:** short and smooth coats **[S1][S4]**. It's also used on wiry coats **[S3]**.
**Limitation:** it isn't designed to get through long or tangled coats.

### Undercoat rake

**What it does:** reaches into a thick undercoat to remove loose undercoat hair **[S2][S3]**.
**Suits:** double coats, alongside your regular brush **[S2][S3]**.
**Limitation:** it's for double coats. Smooth, single-layer coats don't have an undercoat for it to work on.

### Comb

**What it does:** a steel comb lets you check your brushing has reached the skin, finish the coat and remove debris **[S2][S3]**. A wide-toothed steel comb helps work gently through tangles **[S2]**.
**Suits:** most coats that are long enough to tangle, especially long, silky, curly and double coats **[S2][S3]**.
**Limitation:** avoid combs with bent teeth, which can snag or damage the coat **[S2]**.

## De-shedding tools

**What they do:** de-shedding tools, like undercoat rakes, remove loose undercoat. They're used for double coats, gently, in addition to regular brushing **[S3]**.

**Are they safe?** Used gently and as directed, they're part of normal home care for double coats **[S3]**. Like any tool that works close to the skin, they can irritate it if pressed hard or used for too long on one spot.
- **Use light pressure.**
- **Follow the manufacturer's instructions.**
- **Stop if the skin looks red or your dog seems uncomfortable.** If the skin stays irritated, see [problems after grooming](/dog-grooming-problems/).

**Not for:** short and smooth coats, which don't have an undercoat to remove. A rubber curry brush is the usual tool for those coats **[S1][S4]**.

## Bathing and drying equipment

**What you need** (how to bathe: see [how to bathe your dog](/bathing-your-dog/)):
- **Shampoo made for dogs.** Human hair products aren't designed for pets **[S9]**, and dog care organizations recommend dog-specific products **[S1][S3]**.
- **A towel.** A microfiber towel absorbs well **[S3]**.
- **A non-slip mat** for the tub or shower, so your dog feels steady.

**Is a dryer worth it?** A dryer is optional **[S3]**.
- **Many dogs dry well enough** with towels and a warm room.
- **If you use one,** choose a dryer with a cool or low setting. Hot settings can burn a dog's skin **[S9][S3]**.
- **Dryers made for dogs** are used for some coat types **[S1]**.
- **High-powered professional dryers are a groomer's tool.** They're not needed for home bathing.

## Nail tools

How to trim: see [how to trim your dog's nails](/trimming-dog-nails/).

*[Table]*

| Tool | What it does | Often suits | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scissor- or plier-style clippers** | Two blades cross to cut the nail **[S7]** | Plier styles for larger, thicker nails; scissor styles for small dogs **[S6]** | Use clippers made for dogs **[S5]** |
| **Guillotine-style clippers** | The nail goes through a hole and a single blade cuts it **[S6]** | Some owners prefer them **[S5]** | Some veterinary guidance advises against them because they can crush the nail **[S7]**. Other dog organizations list them as a normal option **[S5][S6]**. |
| **Grinder** | Wears the nail down gradually **[S6]** | Thicker nails, or dogs that dislike the feel of clippers **[S6]** | Noisy, which some dogs find frightening **[S6]**. Keep clippers too, for spots a grinder can't reach **[S7]**. |
| **Styptic powder** | Helps stop bleeding if you cut a nail too short **[S5]** | Every nail kit **[S5]** | — |

Both clippers and grinders are safe when used correctly. Choose the one you're most comfortable with and that your dog tolerates best **[S5]**.

## Clippers, blades and scissors

You only need these if you'll do trims at home. What's realistic, and how: see [trimming at home](/dog-haircuts-and-trimming/).

**Scissors:** choose **blunt-ended (round-tip) scissors** for home trims around the face, feet and sanitary area **[S3]**. Pointed grooming shears are a professional's tool.

**Clippers:**
- **For home tidy-ups,** choose **small clippers with guide combs** **[S3]**. Guide combs clip onto the blade to control how long the hair is left **[S8]**.
- **Detachable blades** let you swap in a cool blade if one gets hot **[S8]**.
- **Comfort:** choose clippers that feel comfortable in your hand **[S2]**.

*[Safety callout: "Stop and reassess"]*
- **Blades heat up:** clipper blades can get hot during use. Check them often **[S8]**.
- **Damaged equipment:** never use a damaged or broken blade or comb, because it can injure the skin **[S8]**.
- **Tight mats:** don't buy clippers or scissors to deal with tight mats yourself. That's a job for a groomer (see [dealing with mats](/brushing-your-dog/#dealing-with-mats)).

## Ear and dental supplies

**For ears** (how to clean: see [cleaning your dog's ears](/cleaning-dog-ears/)):
- **A dog ear-cleaning solution,** ideally one your vet recommends **[S10][S11]**.
- **Real cotton balls or gauze** **[S10][S11]**.
- **No cotton swabs or pointed tools.** They can push debris deeper and injure the ear **[S10][S11]**.
- **No hydrogen peroxide.** The American Kennel Club advises against using it in dogs' ears **[S10]**.

**For teeth** (how to brush: see [brushing your dog's teeth](/brushing-dog-teeth/)):
- **A soft-bristled toothbrush.** Many owners start with a finger before moving to a brush **[S12]**.
- **A toothpaste made for pets,** without added fluoride, never human toothpaste **[S12]**.
- **Not needed:** dental scrapers or scaling tools. Hardened tartar is a job for your vet.

## Starter kits by coat type

Everything below builds on a **basic kit for every dog**:
- nail clippers or a grinder, plus styptic powder **[S3][S5]**;
- dog shampoo and a towel **[S1][S3]**;
- a soft toothbrush and pet toothpaste **[S3][S12]**;
- a vet-recommended ear cleaner and cotton balls **[S10][S11]**.

Then add the brushing tools for your dog's coat. These are typical starting points, not rules. Individual dogs vary, and your groomer may suggest something different.

*[Table]*

| Coat group | Brushing tools to start with | Optional extras |
|---|---|---|
| **Short and smooth** | Rubber curry brush **[S1][S3][S4]**; bristle brush **[S1][S4]** | — |
| **Wiry** | Rubber-bristled brush **[S3]**; comb | Ask a groomer how the coat is best kept |
| **Double** | Undercoat rake or de-shedding tool **[S3]**; slicker brush for dense areas **[S2]**; steel comb **[S2]** | Wide-toothed comb for tangles **[S2]** |
| **Long and silky** | Slicker or rubber-bristled brush, plus a steel comb **[S3]**; a pin brush suits many long coats **[S1]** | Blunt-ended scissors or small clippers with guide combs for home tidy-ups **[S3]** |
| **Curly** | Slicker or rubber-bristled brush, plus a steel comb **[S3]** | Blunt-ended scissors or small clippers with guide combs for home tidy-ups **[S3]** |

Not sure which row is your dog? See [identify your dog's coat first](/dog-coat-types/). Comparing the cost of home tools with professional grooming? See [what grooming costs](/dog-grooming-cost/). *[Builder: render this GD-16 link only once GD-16 is published (PC-04).]*

## Common questions

*[FAQ: Elementor Accordion with FAQ Schema ON; question titles set to H3. Rank Math FAQ schema OFF.]*

### Do I need a slicker brush and an undercoat rake?

Only if your dog has a double coat. For double coats, an undercoat rake or de-shedding tool works alongside regular brushing **[S3]**, and a slicker helps in the densest areas **[S2]**. Most other coats need a slicker or a rubber brush plus a comb, but not a rake **[S3]**.

### Is a dog grooming dryer worth it?

A dryer is optional **[S3]**. Towel-drying and a warm room are often enough. If you do use a dryer, choose one with a cool or low setting, because hot dryers can burn a dog's skin **[S9][S3]**. Dryers made for dogs are used for some coat types **[S1]**. See [how to bathe your dog](/bathing-your-dog/) for drying technique.

### What should a beginner buy first?

Start with:
- a brush that suits your dog's coat;
- a comb;
- dog nail clippers or a grinder, with styptic powder;
- dog shampoo and a towel;
- a soft toothbrush with pet toothpaste **[S3][S5][S12]**.

Add other tools only when you have a job for them.
