# How to bathe your dog

*[Intent line]* You want to wash your dog without irritating their skin or eyes, and get them properly dry afterward. Here's how to prepare, bathe, rinse and dry, step by step.

*[Byline]* By Grooming Dogs · Updated *[publish date]*

*[Scope note]* How often to bathe depends on your dog's coat. See [how often to bathe your dog](/how-often-should-you-groom-your-dog/). If your dog fights bath time, start with [if your dog hates the bath](/grooming-an-anxious-dog/).

*[Short answer box]*
**Short answer:**
1. Brush your dog first, because water makes mats worse.
2. Wet the coat with warm water.
3. Work in a shampoo made for dogs from the neck backward, keeping it out of the eyes and ears.
4. Rinse until no shampoo is left.
5. Towel-dry, then use a dryer on a cool or low setting if you need one, held away from the skin.
6. Wash the face separately with a damp cloth.

---

## Before the bath

**Brush first.** Water makes mats worse **[S2]**. Wet mats tighten as they dry and pull on the skin **[S3]**. Brush the coat out completely before your dog gets wet. If you find tight mats, don't bathe over them. See [brush out the coat first](/brushing-your-dog/) or ask a groomer.

**Set up the space.** Choose a warm room, and get everything ready before you bring your dog in, so you never have to leave them alone in the tub. A non-slip mat in the bath or shower helps your dog feel steady. Don't bathe your dog outdoors when the weather is cool or cold: it can cause a dangerous drop in body temperature **[S1]**.

*[What you'll need box. If affiliate links are added later, the inline disclosure line goes at the top of this box, before the first link.]*

**What you'll need**
- Shampoo made for dogs
- Towels (a microfiber towel absorbs well)
- A non-slip mat for the tub or shower
- A cup, jug or handheld shower to wet and rinse
- A soft cloth or cotton balls for the face
- A hair dryer with a cool or low setting (optional)
- Treats

Choosing a dryer or other equipment? See [choosing a dryer and bathing kit](/dog-grooming-tools/).

## Bathing your dog, step by step

*[Numbered steps]*

1. **Wet the coat thoroughly with warm water** **[S1]**. Use comfortably warm water, never hot, and wet right down to the skin. Leave the head for last, or wash it separately (see below).
2. **Apply a mild shampoo made for dogs, working from the neck backward** **[S1]**. Keep shampoo out of your dog's eyes and ears **[S2]**.
3. **Massage the shampoo through the coat** **[S1]**, down to the skin, including the chest, belly, legs and tail.
4. **Rinse completely** (see the next section).
5. **Towel-dry before your dog leaves the bath area,** then finish drying (see "Drying your dog").

Calm, steady handling and a few treats help. If your dog panics or struggles, stop, and see [grooming a dog who hates being groomed](/grooming-an-anxious-dog/) before you try again.

---

## Rinsing thoroughly

Rinse until there's no shampoo left in the coat. Leftover shampoo can irritate the skin **[S1]**. Rinsing usually takes longer than the wash itself, so keep going until the water runs clear and the coat no longer feels slippery. Pay extra attention to:
- the armpits, belly and between the back legs;
- under the tail;
- around the neck and collar area.

If your dog is itchy or smells off after a bath, see [itching or smell after a bath](/dog-grooming-problems/).

## Drying your dog

Drying matters as much as washing. Your dog should be fully dry before going outside in cool weather or settling down for the night.

### Towel drying

Start with a towel **[S3]**. Press and squeeze the water out of the coat rather than rubbing hard, which can tangle long or curly hair. A microfiber towel absorbs well **[S2]**.

### Using a dryer safely

A hair dryer is optional. If you use one:
- **Towel-dry first,** then switch to a cool setting **[S3]** or a low setting **[S2]**. Hot dryers can burn a dog's skin **[S3]**.
- **Keep the dryer moving** back and forth, and hold it away from your dog's skin **[S2]**.
- **Go by your dog's coat and comfort** when deciding whether to use a dryer at all **[S1]**. If the noise upsets your dog, towel-dry and keep them somewhere warm instead.

*[Safety callout: "Stop and reassess"]* Never point a hot dryer at your dog's skin, and never leave a dryer running on a dog unattended.

### Making sure your dog dries fully

Thick and long coats can take a while to dry all the way down to the skin. Keep your dog somewhere warm until they're completely dry. What your dog's coat needs is explained in [dog coat types](/dog-coat-types/).

## Choosing a shampoo

**Use a shampoo made for dogs.** Dog care organizations consistently recommend dog-specific products **[S1][S2]**. Here's why human shampoo isn't a good substitute **[S3]**:
- Human hair products aren't designed for pets.
- Your dog will lick their coat, and human shampoo isn't made with that in mind.
- Some ingredients in human shampoos, such as tea tree oil or anti-dandruff ingredients, may be too harsh for a dog's skin.

**What about baby shampoo?** If you've run out of dog shampoo and have to bathe your dog now, the Animal Medical Center advises using only a no-tears baby product **[S3]**. Treat it as a one-off. Switch back to a dog shampoo for regular baths **[S1]**.

*[See-a-vet callout]* **Medicated shampoos:** use these only if your vet recommends them, and follow the vet's instructions.

**One dirty patch?** You don't always need a full bath. For a muddy paw or a single dirty spot, a spot cleaner, waterless shampoo or wipe made for pets may be enough **[S3]**.

## Washing the face and around the ears

Faces need a gentler approach than the body.

- **Use a damp cloth or cotton ball** to wipe the face and around the eyes, rather than pouring water or shampoo over the head **[S2]**.
- **Keep shampoo and water out of the eyes and ears,** and never push anything into them **[S2]**. Shampoo in the eyes can cause irritation, or a painful injury to the surface of the eye **[S3]**.
- **For dogs with facial folds or wrinkles,** such as Pugs and Shar-Peis, clean the folds gently with damp cotton and dry thoroughly between them **[S4]**.

*[See-a-vet callout]*
- **Eyes:** if your dog is squinting, keeps an eye closed, or the eye looks red or sore after a bath, contact your vet.
- **Ears:** watch for redness, discharge, an unusual smell or pawing at the ears, especially in dogs with floppy ears. Contact a vet if you notice them **[S2]**.

Routine ear care is covered in [cleaning your dog's ears](/cleaning-dog-ears/).

## Common questions

*[FAQ: Elementor Accordion with FAQ Schema ON. Rank Math FAQ schema OFF for this page.]*

**How do I bathe my dog without a bathtub?**
A walk-in shower with a handheld showerhead and a non-slip mat works well, and small dogs can often be washed in a large sink. Outdoors, use a hose only in warm weather: bathing outdoors in cool or cold weather can dangerously lower your dog's body temperature **[S1]**. For a single dirty spot, a pet spot cleaner or waterless shampoo may do instead of a full bath **[S3]**.

**Should I bathe my dog before or after a haircut?**
Before. Brush, bathe and dry your dog thoroughly first, then trim **[S2]**. A clean, dry, brushed coat shows you what actually needs trimming. See [trimming and haircuts at home](/dog-haircuts-and-trimming/).

**Can I use a hair dryer on my dog?**
Yes, with care. Towel-dry first, then use a cool or low setting **[S2][S3]**. Keep the dryer moving and away from the skin, because a hot dryer can burn **[S2][S3]**. If your dog is frightened by the noise, towel-dry and keep them warm instead.
